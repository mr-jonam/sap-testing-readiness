SAP testing: questions before a change

What business evidence should be discussed for the affected SAP journey?

The questions below are original editorial prompts. They are not a vendor-prescribed checklist, a validated assessment model or implementation instructions.

01 / Discussion focus

Use the business process as the discussion boundary

For an SAP change, begin with the business outcome and affected process owner. Order-to-cash and procure-to-pay are illustrative process families, not supplied test packs. Different landscapes can place their start and end points in different products.

Question to bring: Which process outcome is changing, and who can judge it?

02 / Discussion focus

Discuss roles, interfaces and timing

A visible screen is one part of a journey. Ask whether role-dependent behaviour, external handovers and scheduled processing matter to the change. These are scoping prompts; they do not assert that a particular implementation has any of these risks.

Question to bring: Which role or handover could change the business outcome?

03 / Discussion focus

Locate platform evidence without assuming coverage

SAP publishes a Cloud ALM Test Management documentation entry point. Confirm available functionality, version, entitlement and landscape fit in the current documentation. A product capability should not be presented as evidence that a customer journey has been accepted.

Question to bring: Where would a business owner find understandable acceptance evidence?

04 / Discussion focus

Make uncertainty explicit before requesting delivery

A focused first conversation can use the change category, process family, broad deadline and open evidence questions. Detailed landscape exports, transports, credentials and production data belong outside a public enquiry.

Question to bring: Which unanswered question would most affect the release discussion?

Continue with your own context

Keep the public discussion at the level of business outcomes and broad change categories. A detailed approach depends on a separately agreed scope and authorised access.
